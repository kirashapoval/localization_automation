5 Psychology Lessons You Must Know

ARTICLE BY

Jason Douson

- DECEMBER 23

If you’ve clicked on this article, chances are you have at least a casual interest in psychology, and it’s fantastic that you’re dedicating your time to learning about the human condition. Kudos for that!

Studying psychology can be a rewarding experience for anyone, and it’s never too late to start. However, we’d all be much better off if we could have these important lessons in school. Think of how much easier your life would have been! So, in this article, we’re going to discuss 5 simple but life-changing truths we all should’ve learned during our formative and impressionable years.

1. Not everything that happens is about you

Source: Shutterstock

Kids, and especially teenagers, tend to perceive the surrounding world in a very dramatic and simplistic way. The smallest events can be a matter of life and death to them, and everything has to mean something to them personally. Unfortunately, many of us carry that attitude well into adulthood.

You know that meme about being the main character of your life? Of course, it’s nice to feel like you’re the protagonist of your own story. But there’s a key thing to remember here: not everything that happens to you about you, and guess what? It’s for the better.

It’s much less exhausting to go through life knowing that sometimes, things just happen. Our egocentric biases and reactiveness make us assign some special meaning to events that would’ve just passed us by if we didn’t pay attention. Understanding this will help you not take things close to the heart or worry about meaningless stuff.

2. You need to take responsibility for your life

And by responsibility, we mean taking charge of your thoughts, actions, decisions, and feelings. You can’t expect other people to accommodate your needs at your slightest whim, and neither can you blame them for refusing or not being able to meet those needs. Yes, it’s a bitter pill to swallow, but the sooner you realize this simple truth, the better.

Here’s what you can do. First of all, understand that no one is obliged to put you first. The only person who can do it 100% of the time is you, so make yourself a priority and think of how you can meet your needs on your own. Still, that doesn’t mean you should never ever get help from other people. It’s more about becoming more self-reliant and self-sufficient.

3. Internal validation is just as important as external validation

Source: Shutterstock

One of the inherent problems of modern education, especially at the school level, is that it’s almost entirely based on external validation. If you do a good job, you get a high grade and some positive feedback from your teacher, which is nice, but it’s not enough.

We all need internal validation to build a strong and healthy self-image, and it’s unfortunate that most schools don’t teach kids how to achieve it. Instead, they’re conditioned to rely on external validation for self-esteem.

4. You shouldn’t care about how others perceive you

Humans are social creatures, so it’s in our nature to long for acceptance and to care about what others think of us. But as painful as it is to realize, you can’t force other people to like you. Sure, you can make a positive impression or try to improve the relationship, but ultimately, it’s up to them to decide what they think of you.

If you really want to feel accepted, you should first accept yourself. Understanding this will save you tons of time and energy you’d waste trying to please other people without any actual fulfillment. Basically, you have minimal control over how other people perceive you, and there’s nothing you can do about it. So, just let go.

5. Being assertive has little to do with aggression

Source: Shutterstock

Remember those bullies that used to rule the schoolyard with fist and terror? They might have tried to seem assertive and strong, but the truth is that you can’t achieve true assertiveness through aggression. It must come from a positive place of inner strength.

Interestingly enough, bullies often use aggression to cover up their own insecurities and make others feel worse than they do. In contrast, true assertiveness is about standing up for what you want and need while at the same time recognizing and respecting the wants and needs of others.

You live and you learn

It’s truly mind-boggling that psychology still isn’t a part of the curriculum in most high schools. One would argue that it’s as essential for our general education as trigonometry and physics because it teaches us about how the human mind works. We need this knowledge in order to live happier and healthier, build strong relationships with other people, and basically, live in harmony with ourselves.

Let’s hope that in the future, school kids will be taught all these simple but vital truths, but for now, we must do what we can to educate ourselves. Keep learning, and may it help you become a better version of yourself!
